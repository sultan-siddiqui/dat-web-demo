Islamic finance, how it works

Murabaha vs. Ijarah, side by side.

The main difference: with Murabaha the property is registered in your name immediately. With Ijarah you rent it from the financier until the purchase price is repaid in full.

Our program

Murabaha

A purchase contract with a fixed profit rate that will not change over the life of the contract.

  • Deed in your name from day one
  • Fixed profit rate and instalment
  • Both parties know the original purchase price
  • Profit is known and agreed upon
Alternative

Ijarah

A lease contract with a profit rate that can change. Ownership transfers at the end of the term.

  • You rent the property during the term
  • Rate can be variable
  • Ownership transfers once fully repaid
  • Two contracts: lease with promise of ownership

Types of Islamic home finance

Five structures. One principle.

All avoid interest by tying finance to a real asset or a real partnership.

What we offerMurabaha

The financier buys the property and immediately sells it to you at a disclosed profit. You pay fixed monthly instalments with no interest.

Ijara

The financier buys the property and leases it to you, transferring ownership when the term ends.

Musharaka

You and the financier buy together, and you gradually buy out its share.

Mudarabah

A partnership: one partner provides capital, the other manages the investment.

Wakala

The financier acts as your agent, investing your money in Shariah-compliant trade for a target profit.

Forbidden home loans

Loans that involve interest (riba) are not permissible except in cases of urgent necessity, and buying a house does not reach that level because the need can be met by renting. You can rest assured that Dar Al Tamleek does not offer any riba-based loans.
